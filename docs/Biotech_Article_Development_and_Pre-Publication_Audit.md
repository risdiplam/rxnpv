## Stage 1 — Article Blueprint

Do not draft prose until you first provide the proposed article architecture.

Use the following sections when relevant:

1. **Executive Thesis** — what the company is attempting to do; what evidence establishes today; why the idea may be underappreciated, overappreciated, or unresolved; the next decisive proof point.
2. **Disease Biology and Current Treatment Gap** — disease mechanism, natural history, standard of care, and unmet need; clearly distinguish prevalence from realistically treatable population.
3. **Therapeutic Hypothesis** — mechanism, target, modality, and why intervention may address disease biology; key translational uncertainty.
4. **Preclinical and Translational Evidence** — what models show; what remains unproven in humans; major relevance and reproducibility limitations.
5. **Human Evidence and Trial Interpretation** — trial design, endpoints, patient population, efficacy, safety, dose response, durability, and missing data; explicitly identify uncontrolled, exploratory, or post hoc components.
6. **Regulatory Path** — potential approval path, endpoint/surrogate considerations, precedent, and principal regulatory risk.
7. **Competitive Landscape** — approved care, direct competitors, differentiated mechanisms, timing, and competitive threats.
8. **Financial Position and Catalysts** — cash/runway, financing risk, key expected events, and principal execution risks. Do not overemphasize valuation if a separate valuation tool is used. If RxNPV was used to compute a valuation, state its output as a sourced conclusion (e.g., "RxNPV's base case implies $X per share, versus a current price of $Y") with a one-line note on the driving assumption, rather than re-deriving or narrating the DCF mechanics in prose.
9. **Risks and Alternative Interpretation** — strongest bear case; alternative explanation for favorable data; what would invalidate the thesis.
10. **Bottom Line** — evidence-based judgment; appropriate uncertainty; most appropriate next action: continue diligence, monitor, wait for catalyst, deprioritize, or reject.
11. **References** — full citations and direct links where the publication platform permits.

## Stage 2 — Drafting Rules

After blueprint approval:

- Draft in clear, scientifically precise language.
- Use citations directly after material factual claims.
- Use the Fact / Inference / Speculation distinction internally; in published prose, make inferential language clear without mechanically labeling every sentence.
- Do not repeat the company's narrative without independent analysis.
- Do not overstate preliminary evidence.
- Explain technical concepts where needed, but do not dilute scientific accuracy.
- Include meaningful limitations beside favorable evidence rather than isolating them in a final disclaimer.
- Preserve the difference between biological plausibility, clinical activity, and confirmed clinical benefit.
- Use direct source links wherever your publication format supports them.

---

## Stage 3 — Pre-Publication Fact-Check and Red-Team Audit

After the draft is complete, audit it as a skeptical translational scientist, clinical reviewer, FDA reviewer, and biotech investor.

For every material sentence or table claim:

1. Identify whether it is Fact, Inference, or Speculation.
2. Confirm whether the cited source directly supports the claim.
3. Flag citations that are secondary, outdated, weak, inaccessible, company-sponsored, conference-only, or otherwise limited.
4. Flag causal claims derived from uncontrolled, open-label, small-N, biomarker-only, subgroup, or post hoc data.
5. Flag overstated mechanism, efficacy, safety, regulatory, epidemiology, commercial, competitor, or financial language.
6. Identify omitted evidence or caveats that would materially change interpretation.
7. Identify company claims treated as though independently verified.
8. Check whether the draft fairly presents the strongest contrary argument.
9. Check all numbers, dates, trial identifiers, endpoints, denominators, p-values, confidence intervals, and regulatory terminology.
10. Confirm that direct links work and point to the intended source.

**E. Publication Decision** — conclude with one of:

- **Ready to publish:** No material factual, citation, or balance problems remain.
- **Ready after minor revisions:** List precise required edits.
- **Requires substantial revision:** Identify missing evidence, unsupported conclusions, or structural flaws.
- **Do not publish yet:** Explain why the article's thesis exceeds available evidence.

Do not rewrite the entire article unless explicitly requested. Propose the minimum precise edits needed to make it scientifically rigorous and publication-ready.
